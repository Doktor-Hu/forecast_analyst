## Business Case Opening

"My recommendation is to give the factory a base January forecast of 856k cases. That reflects the most recent clean demand signal after correcting delivered actuals for casefill and separating promotion effects from normal demand. I would not load Sales' upside into the factory baseline until the promotion is real: customer, dates, mechanic, display support, weekly phasing, committed volume, and fallback plan. If those are signed, I would move to about 1,144k cases. I would keep 1,246k as an upside capacity scenario, not the default supply signal."

## Suggested 8 Minute Flow

1. Role framing, 45 seconds
   - "My forecast informs the factory, so I need a number supply can act on."
   - "Sales can have a commercial ambition, but the operational forecast needs signed assumptions."

2. Data treatment, 90 seconds
   - Actual deliveries understate ordered demand when casefill is below 100%.
   - I estimate ordered demand from delivered actuals and casefill.
   - I split promotion and non-promotion demand before using history for January.
   - I treat service-affected weeks as a risk signal rather than true demand weakness.

3. Key evidence, 2 minutes
   - 2021 forecast bias was about -13%, so history needs correction.
   - MAPE was about 40%, driven by promotion volatility and service disruption.
   - There were 14 promotion weeks and 38 non-promotion weeks.
   - Recent clean weekly demand was around 212k cases median.
   - P13 estimated ordered demand was 856k cases, a stronger anchor than a blind annual average.

4. Recommendation, 90 seconds
   - Factory baseline: 856k cases.
   - Signed two-week promotion: 1,144k cases.
   - Sales upside sensitivity: 1,246k cases.
   - I would publish one official forecast and one conditional upside gate, not blend them into a vague middle number.

5. Alignment plan, 90 seconds
   - I would show Sales that I am not rejecting growth; I am defining what must be true to operationalize it.
   - Ask for customer, mechanic, dates, display support, price depth, expected weekly phasing, committed orders, cannibalization view, and contingency if casefill risk appears.
   - Use a sign-off table: base, signed promo, upside sensitivity.

6. Follow-up, 45 seconds
   - Lock a weekly demand-sensing check through January.
   - Compare order intake, customer commitments, casefill, and sell-out if available.
   - Update the factory only when gates are met.
   - Run a post-promotion review to improve the next forecast.

## Strong Closing

"The core point is that I would not turn uncertainty into one artificial number. I would give Supply Chain a reliable base forecast, give Sales a clear path to unlock upside, and make the promotion decision visible through signed assumptions."

## Likely Business Case Questions

Why not use Sales' January number directly?
- Because the factory forecast should represent supply risk and committed demand, not ambition alone. I would support Sales with scenarios, but only move the operational baseline after the promotion assumptions are signed.

Why is 856k a reasonable baseline?
- It is anchored in the most recent clean demand period and corrects for service distortion. It is also consistent with the idea that recent demand is a better short-term signal than the noisy full-year average.

Why not average base and promo scenarios?
- Averaging hides the real decision. Either the promotion exists with mechanics and customer support, or it does not. A blended number can create supply without commercial commitment.

What extra information do you need most?
- Customer, promotion mechanic, display support, price depth, dates, weekly phasing, committed orders, expected cannibalization, inventory and casefill constraints, and the fallback plan.

How would you get Sales on board?
- I would frame it as "yes, if." The base forecast protects service and waste; the promo gate gives Sales a clear path to a higher number. I would ask them to co-own the assumptions and sign the scenario.

What if Sales insists on the upside?
- I would show the upside as a capacity and risk scenario, then ask what evidence supports it: customer commitment, display plan, promotional mechanics, and early order signals. If they cannot evidence it, I would not load it as the factory baseline.

What are the limitations of your analysis?
- One year of history, uneven promotion mechanics, no price/display detail, no sell-out data, and service disruptions. That is why I use scenarios and gates rather than overclaiming precision.

What do you expect Sales will do?
- They will likely push for the higher number because they see momentum and want supply secured. I would expect them to challenge the baseline, so I would redirect the discussion to the decision gates.

## SWOT Speaker Track

Strengths:
- "My main strength is translating messy demand data into decisions people can actually sign off. In this case I did not just model the data; I separated base demand, promotion demand, and service distortion so the forecast can be used operationally."

Weaknesses:
- "A development area is category and customer-specific intuition. I can build a solid first view from data, but I would actively seek input from sales, category, and supply colleagues to calibrate mechanics like display support and price depth."

Opportunities:
- "The opportunity I see is to bring more structured demand-sensing into the business: clearer promo gates, better forecast learning loops, and dashboards that help Sales and Supply Chain work from the same facts."

Threats:
- "The main threat is conflicting incentives. Sales may want upside secured early, while Supply Chain needs a reliable signal. I manage that by separating ambition from signed assumptions and by making risks visible before the number is locked."

## Questions To Ask Them

1. "How does Mars currently distinguish commercial ambition from the forecast that is sent to Supply Chain?"
2. "What information usually improves promotional forecast quality most here: customer commitments, sell-out, display compliance, or historical mechanics?"
3. "How are forecast analysts expected to challenge Sales while still keeping a collaborative relationship?"
4. "What would success look like in the first 90 days for this Forecast Analyst role?"

## Final Rehearsal Checklist

- Can explain the 856k recommendation in one sentence.
- Can explain why 1,144k is conditional, not rejected.
- Can name the extra information needed without looking down.
- Can defend the difference between Sales forecast and factory forecast.
- Can present SWOT in less than 3 minutes.
- Can admit limitations without sounding uncertain.
